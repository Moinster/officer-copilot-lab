Welcome and objectives

A practical lab for senior leaders: copy, adapt, and use executive-ready Microsoft 365 Copilot prompts against the work you already own — brand and category performance, customer and channel signals, supply and service issues, and the decisions that follow. Use your own work context wherever possible; the fallback demo data is fictional.

Catch up

Run a weekly catch-up across email, Teams chats, meetings, and files without reading everything.

Prepare

Walk into a customer, brand, or operations review with context, positions, and questions in minutes.

Decide

Extract decisions, action items, owners, dates, and open questions from what already happened.

Analyze

Surface trends, risks, and growth opportunities from category, customer, and consumer data.

Activity 2

The prompting pattern

Strong prompts give Copilot a clear goal, relevant context, the sources it should use, and clear expectations for the response. The pattern matters more than the wording.

GoalContextSourceExpectations

Worked example

Identify an impactful active initiative I have been working on and prepare an executive summary for an upcoming leadership discussion. The audience is a cross-functional leadership team spanning brand, sales, supply chain, and finance; the discussion is limited to 15 minutes; and the objective is to align on one decision or next step. Review email threads and Teams messages from the past two weeks where I am a sender or recipient. Prioritize recent developments and unresolved issues, and cite the messages supporting each finding. Begin with a brief explanation of why you selected this initiative based on its activity and potential impact. Then provide three key points, the decision needed, two risks, and a recommended next action.
  • State the goal clearly: what do you want Copilot to accomplish?
  • Add context about the audience, situation, people, brand, customer, or time period.
  • Identify the email, meeting, file, or dataset Copilot should use.
  • Set expectations for format, tone, length, and what to prioritize.
  • Verify facts, numbers, and names before acting or sharing.
Try it now: Run the worked example as written, then rerun it with one change — swap the audience for a customer-facing team or a single functional leader. Notice how much the output shifts on context alone.
Activity 3 · 7 minutes

Catch up: find the signal.

Use Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat to synthesize recent work context, then narrow the response to decisions and risks.

Start a new chat before running this exercise so context from the worked example does not influence the response.
  1. Set your scope. Replace the bracketed areas with the brands, customers, functions, or stakeholders you own.
  2. Run the prompt. Review the sources Copilot provides and open at least one supporting item.
  3. Challenge the result. Ask what is uncertain, contradictory, or missing.

Leadership signal scan

Review my work from the past five business days related to [brands, customers, supply chain, marketing, people, finance, or another area I own]. Identify the five developments most likely to require executive attention. For each, explain what changed, why it matters now, the decision or intervention that may be needed, and the source supporting the finding. Separate confirmed facts from possible implications.

Pressure-test follow-up

Which of these findings has the weakest evidence or conflicting signals? Show me what I should verify before acting.

Optional · Narrow to one customer or brand

Now restrict the scan to everything related to [customer or brand] in the past two weeks. Show what changed, who is driving it, what is blocked and on whom, and the single decision most likely to be asked of me next.

Reflection: Did Copilot surface a decision, or merely organize information?

Activity 4 · 7 minutes

Prepare: enter informed.

Use Copilot with an upcoming meeting and the content available to you in Teams or Outlook. The goal is not a generic agenda—it is a sharper leadership posture.

Start a new chat before adding the meeting so the preparation is grounded only in the meeting and its related work context.
Point Copilot to the meeting: In Copilot Chat, make sure Work IQ is on. Select Add work content in the prompt box, or type /. Choose Meetings, search by meeting title or organizer, and select the meeting. It will appear as a reference in the prompt.
  1. Choose a real upcoming meeting. A customer business review, S&OP checkpoint, brand plan review, or leadership staff meeting works well. Add it to the prompt using the Work IQ steps above.
  2. Run the preparation prompt. Attach the meeting reference before you send it.
  3. Verify participant positions. Confirm them against the source content before treating them as fact.

Leadership meeting pre-read

Using the meeting I added to this prompt, prepare me for the discussion. Summarize the purpose, recent developments connected to the agenda, and any decisions that appear to be needed. Identify the participants whose input will be important, but do not infer a person's position unless it is supported by available content. End with three questions I should ask and two assumptions the group should test.

Optional · Customer business review lens

Reframe this preparation for a customer business review. Organize it as: what we committed last time, what actually happened, what the customer is likely to raise, where our position is weakest, and the one ask I should make. Flag anything I would be asserting without evidence in the available content.

Engagement moment: Pair up and compare the questions Copilot proposed. Which one would materially improve the meeting?

Activity 5 · 12 minutes

Analyze: test the story.

Move from communication signals to business evidence. Choose one route; each uses a different dataset and a prompt designed for that tool and business question.

Copilot in ExcelUse the workbook directly in Excel to compare regions, retail customers, and category platforms.

  • Download and open the performance workbook in Excel.
  • Select the Copilot button near the upper-right corner to open the Copilot pane.
  • Start a new conversation in the pane, then paste the prompt below.

Copilot in Excel prompt

Analyze this weekly customer and category performance data to identify where leadership attention would have the greatest impact this week. Compare regions, retail customers, and category platforms; identify meaningful outliers and trends across net sales, volume, service level, and on-shelf availability; and show the evidence behind each finding. Recommend three actions and the additional information needed before making a final decision. Create a visualization that makes the most important comparison easy to understand.

AnalystUse the Analyst agent in Microsoft 365 Copilot to identify recurring themes and intervention priorities in consumer feedback.

  • Open the Microsoft 365 Copilot app and select Analyst under Agents.
  • Select the + attachment control and add the consumer care feedback data.
  • Start a new Analyst conversation, then paste the prompt below.

Analyst prompt

Analyze this consumer care feedback data to identify the issues most likely to require executive intervention. Group comments into recurring themes, quantify how often each theme appears, identify the brands, category platforms, regions, retail customers, or purchase moments most affected, and include representative evidence. Recommend the three intervention priorities with the expected consumer or operational benefit of each. Clearly identify limitations or missing context in the data.

Optional · Bring your own data

Analyze the attached [workbook or CSV] to help me decide [the business decision]. The data covers [time period, population, and business process]. First describe the fields and any data-quality limitations you observe. Then identify the most important trends, exceptions, risks, and opportunities; support each finding with evidence; and recommend three actions. Clearly distinguish what the data proves from what would require additional context.

Pressure-test either route

Argue against your leading recommendation. What alternative explanation, missing data, or risk could change the decision?
Required final step: After reviewing and correcting the analysis, run the handoff prompt below. This creates the common input used in Activity 6, regardless of which route or dataset you chose.

Create the Activity 6 handoff

Create a Decision Brief Handoff from this analysis. Include: the decision question; three findings supported by the source data; the evidence for each finding; the recommended action; two risks or counterarguments; and unresolved questions. Keep it under 350 words. Mark any unsupported or uncertain item as Needs validation.

Executive edit

Reduce this to no more than one page. Lead with the decision required, retain only evidence that materially affects the decision, and end with the recommended owner and next checkpoint.

Optional · Make it presentable

Turn this brief into a five-slide leadership presentation: the decision, the evidence, the options and trade-offs, the recommendation with owner and date, and the risks with mitigations. Keep every slide to a single message with supporting detail in the notes. Do not add data that is not in the brief.

Reflection: Did the brief make the decision easier to understand, or simply make the analysis shorter?

Activity 7 · 6 minutes

Follow through: make it accountable.

Point Copilot Chat to a specific past meeting, convert the discussion into explicit commitments, and open a ready-to-review follow-up draft in Outlook.

Start a new chat before adding the past meeting so Copilot does not carry forward context from the analysis and writing activities.
Add the specific meeting: In Copilot Chat, make sure Work IQ is on. Select Add work content, or type /. Choose Meetings, search by meeting title or organizer, and select the meeting before pasting the prompt.

Decision record and Outlook follow-up

Using the meeting I added to this prompt, create a decision and owner record from the available recap or transcript. Include decisions made, action items with the named owner and stated due date, unresolved questions, and items discussed but not decided. Do not invent an owner or deadline; mark it as unassigned or not specified. Then send an Outlook email to the attendees with the decisions first, owners and dates second, and open questions last. End by asking recipients to correct anything that does not match their understanding.

Open the draft in Outlook

Make the email concise and executive-ready. Use a clear subject line and keep the body under 250 words.
  1. Review the generated email card in Copilot Chat and confirm the recipients, subject, decisions, owners, and dates.
  2. Select Open to continue editing the generated draft in Outlook.
  3. Edit and send only after confirming the draft against the meeting record.
Availability note: Use a meeting that was transcribed or recorded so Copilot can ground the recap and follow-up in the meeting content.
Activity 8 · 5 minutes

Lead responsibly.

Copilot accelerates preparation and production. Executive accountability remains with the person who acts on the output.

VerifyOpen sources and confirm names, dates, numbers, and quotations.
SeparateDistinguish facts, interpretations, assumptions, and recommendations.
ProtectUse content only within its intended permissions and business context.
ChallengeAsk for counterarguments, missing evidence, and alternate explanations.
EditApply your judgment to tone, audience, and organizational implications.
OwnThe decision and communication remain yours—not Copilot's.

Your next move

Choose one recurring executive workflow — the weekly business review, the customer business review cycle, the S&OP escalation, or the monthly brand health read. Redesign it as a sequence: catch up, prepare, analyze, decide, create, and follow through.

Commit to one workflow

Help me redesign one recurring workflow I own: [name the meeting, review, or reporting cycle]. Today it takes [time] and involves [people or teams]. Map it into six stages - catch up, prepare, analyze, decide, create, follow through - and for each stage give me the specific Copilot prompt I would use, the source content it needs, and the human check I must not skip. End with the one stage where I should start next week and why.

Reflection: Where could Copilot improve the quality of a decision—not just the speed of a task?

Activity 9 · 8 minutes

Inspire and lead adoption.

Your own fluency is the starting point, not the finish line. This activity turns what you just practiced into how your organization works, using Microsoft's published adoption guidance for leaders.

Why this sits with you

Microsoft's AI Strategy Roadmap research, drawn from interviews with more than 100 business and IT leaders, is blunt about what separates organizations that get value from AI: “Senior leadership's vision and support are—by far—the strongest drivers of success.”

Source: The AI Strategy Roadmap, Microsoft Cloud Blog.

Microsoft's Copilot adoption frameworkThe five stages Microsoft publishes for AI leaders. Find where your organization actually is before deciding what to do next.

Plan

Define business value and the scenarios worth pursuing first.

Implement

Get licensed users skilled and productive quickly.

Adopt

Build habit and community around real, repeated use.

Manage

Measure usage and impact; govern how AI is used.

Extend & optimize

Move from assisted work to agents and redesigned processes.

Source: Advanced resources for Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption — AI Leaders track, Microsoft Adoption.

What Microsoft asks leaders to doThese are the leader-level practices Microsoft documents, distinct from the work an IT admin or change manager owns.

SponsorProvide visible executive sponsorship and a unified vision of success that ties AI to a shared business imperative—not to a tool rollout.
Build trustMicrosoft's leadership guidance is framed as “It's not about AI. It's about trust.” Treat the human side of enablement as the competitive advantage.
GovernStand up an AI governance council so decisions about acceptable use, data, and risk are made deliberately rather than case by case.
Empower championsName and back a champion network. Champions create the groundswell that increases adoption and reduce strain on the core project team.
UpskillTreat capability building as a workforce strategy. Nearly half of leaders name upskilling existing employees as a top priority for the next 12–18 months.
MeasureUse the Copilot Dashboard and Copilot Analytics to track real usage and impact, then act on what they show.

Sources: Leading in the era of AI, Microsoft 365 Champion Program, and the 2025 Work Trend Index.

The pitfall Microsoft names: leaders who endorse AI in principle without providing a concrete plan and vision. Microsoft's 2024 Work Trend Index found that while leaders agree AI is a business imperative, many believe their organization lacks a plan to move from individual impact to bottom-line results. Enthusiasm without a plan reads to your teams as permission to wait.
  1. Locate yourself on the framework. Decide honestly which of the five stages describes your organization today.
  2. Run the adoption plan prompt. Use the scenario you committed to in Activity 8 as the anchor.
  3. Name three people. Leave this activity with actual names for your champion network, not a role description.

Build your 90-day adoption plan

Act as an adoption strategist. I lead [team or function] of roughly [size] people, and we are at the [Plan / Implement / Adopt / Manage / Extend and optimize] stage of Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption. The workflow I most want to change is [the workflow from Activity 8]. Build me a 90-day adoption plan with three phases. For each phase give me: the outcome I am driving, the two or three actions I personally take as the leader, what I delegate and to whom, the signal that tells me it is working, and the risk that would stall it. Separate what I can start this week from what needs preparation. Keep it to one page.

Identify your champions

Based on my recent emails, chats, and meetings, identify five people in my organization who show signs of being effective AI champions: they experiment with new tools, others come to them for help, and they communicate clearly across teams. For each person, cite the specific evidence that led you to suggest them and note what they would need from me to succeed in that role. Do not infer enthusiasm or skill that is not supported by available content, and tell me where the evidence is thin.

Write the message that sets expectations

Draft a short message from me to [team or organization] about how we will use Microsoft 365 Copilot. Include: why this matters to our business specifically, one concrete example of how I am using it in my own work, what I am asking people to try in the next 30 days, where to get help, and what will not change - our standards for accuracy, judgment, and accountability. Direct and human, under 250 words. No hype and no mandate language.

Close: Your teams will calibrate to what you actually do, not what you announce. What will they see you doing differently next week?
